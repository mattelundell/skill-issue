Skill Issue is a study app that runs on your own computer. It ships with no subjects; a subject is a subject pack of sources, lessons and questions that is loaded into it.

**Lessons.** A subject is a path through areas, and each area has lessons. A lesson is a sequence of steps with one idea in each: text, examples and check questions. Where you are in a lesson is saved at every step, so you continue where you left off. A question you answer wrongly in a lesson is asked once more after the last step.

**Glossary and material.** Words from the subject's glossary are marked in the text, with their explanation beside it. The Material tab collects the sources the lessons rest on.

**Notes.** Beside every step is a box for your own notes. They are saved as you type and collected under the Notes tab, sorted by lesson and searchable.

**Questions.** Questions come in eight kinds: one answer, several answers, true or false, a number with a unit, a short word, an order, pairs to match, and free recall, where you grade yourself. After every answer it says what was right and why.

**Exams.** Every lesson and every area has an exam, drawn from the question banks and new for every attempt. An exam is graded when you hand it in, and passes at a certain share right.

**Review.** Every question you have answered comes back when it is time to refresh it: soon if you answered wrongly, later the better you know it. The Review tab shows what is due today, and the start page gathers it from every subject under Review everything.

**Progress.** The Progress tab shows how much of the subject's questions you remember right now, by area and lesson and over time, and the concepts you most often get wrong. A lesson is mastered when its exam is passed and you remember most of its questions.

**Language and diagnostics.** The app's language is chosen at the top right; a subject's content can be read in the languages the subject has, chosen on the subject's page. Diagnostics in the footer records a study session, if something goes wrong, as a file to pass on.
